# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A personal academic website for Daniel F. Parra, behavioral & experimental
economist (Assistant Professor, PU Javeriana; PhD Berlin School of Economics).
It serves three audiences at once, and each should find their path within
seconds of landing:

- **Academic peers & collaborators** — evaluating the research, looking for
  papers, methods, and a reason to collaborate.
- **Students & mentees** — prospective advisees gauging the work and deciding
  whether to reach out.
- **Journalists & general public** — press and curious readers (the work has
  Psychology Today / WZB coverage) who want the plain-language takeaway.

Not primarily a job-market site: he already holds a faculty position, so the
goal is standing and reach, not candidacy.

## Product Purpose

Present a focused body of research on lying, dishonesty, corruption, and
prosociality — and the institutional conditions that raise or lower the cost of
honesty. Success is a visitor who, in one short session, understands what Daniel
studies, can reach the relevant paper or the CV, and feels invited to make
contact (collaboration, talk invitation, or student inquiry).

## Brand Personality

Warm, approachable, human. The subject matter is serious and somewhat provocative
(lying, corruption), but the voice should invite rather than intimidate. Quiet
expertise that doesn't need to perform its seriousness. Three-word read:
**warm · precise · inviting**. Note the live tension: the current build leans
austere dark/editorial; future work should pull the *feel* toward human warmth
without sacrificing rigor (carried via voice, contrast, and imagery rather than
by abandoning the identity).

## Anti-references

- **Generic university faculty page** — white background, blue underlined links,
  headshot-left, a flat dump of publications. The default institutional template
  is the single thing to avoid. Every reflexive faculty-page move is a cue to do
  something more considered.
- **Google Scholar / ORCID clone** — a pure data listing with no design point of
  view. The papers should read as curated work, not a database export.
- (Also avoid, by extension) startup/SaaS marketing tropes and over-designed
  flashy-portfolio gimmickry that would undercut academic credibility.

## Design Principles

1. **Let the work carry the credibility.** Design supports the research; it never
   upstages it. The papers and their findings are the hero.
2. **Warm authority.** Present serious, edgy subject matter with a human,
   approachable voice. Invite contact; don't intimidate.
3. **Not a faculty page.** Treat every default institutional-template move as a
   prompt to make a more deliberate choice.
4. **Three readers, one page.** A peer, a student, and a journalist should each
   find their path quickly — the abstract-on-expand pattern and clear CTAs serve
   this; protect it.
5. **Restraint over flash.** Distinctiveness comes from craft, typography, and
   precise motion — not gimmicks that would erode academic trust.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 against the dark ground.
`prefers-reduced-motion` is honored (entrance reveals resolve instantly, looping
decoration stops). The minty-green accent must remain legible on near-black and
should not be the sole carrier of meaning. Keyboard-navigable; expandable papers
operable without a pointer.
