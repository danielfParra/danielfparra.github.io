## Overall Impression

The counters are a real, fixable problem, not a taste dispute — both assessments converge on it independently (one from source-reading + brand-doc cross-reference, the other from the deterministic scanner + live screenshot), and it's worth fixing first. But it's one symptom of a slightly bigger pattern: the site over-explains itself with metadata (counts, redundant index numbers, a stats strip that repeats numbers already visible below it) in the one section — Research — that's supposed to feel most curated. Strip the arithmetic, keep the craft (the unfold interaction, the type system, the accessibility work) — those are genuinely strong.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The count badges are a generic tic that breaks the site's own anti-reference and burns its one reserved color.**
- **Why it matters**: `.research-stats` (06 published / 03 working papers / 02 in progress) restates the exact same three integers that `.group-sep .count` restates again at each group boundary — the same numbers, stated up to 3 times, and again countable directly from the numbered list below. Both `.research-stats b` and `.group-sep .count` spend Signal Mint — the color DESIGN.md reserves for "the finding," capped at ≤10% of any screen — on a raw tally, which is definitionally not a finding. And DESIGN.md's own words reject exactly this: *"the Google Scholar / ORCID export (a database listing with no point of view)... the papers here are curated specimens, not rows in a table."* A mono-uppercase label plus a colored numeral is precisely how a database export renders a row count — the site is quietly reproducing the one aesthetic it explicitly promised to avoid, inside its highest-craft section (Research) and its most personal one (CV: "Education · 03 entries" — a CV is not a database table). Nothing scrolls, filters, or paginates off these counts, so they carry zero interactive value.
- **Fix**: Delete `<span class="count">` from all three `.group-sep` divs (lines 461, 517, 635). Delete `.research-stats` entirely, or replace it with an editorial sentence instead of a tally ("Six studies published, three in review, two more underway" — if even that's wanted; deleting outright is also fine given the redundancy). Delete `<span class="num">N entries</span>` from both CV `<h3>`s (674, 699). Consider whether the `/01`–`/11` per-entry index prefixes should stay — they're part of the intentional "specimen" numbering motif rather than a redundant tally, so they're a separate, lower-stakes call; if you want the page free of numbers entirely, those go too.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill` (or hand-edit directly — this is a small, mechanical deletion across 5-6 spots).

**[P2] `.p-abstract em` breaks the site's own Mint Emphasis Rule in the one place it matters most.**
- **Why it matters**: Line 234 sets abstract emphasis as `color:var(--accent); font-style:italic; font-weight:500` — italic AND bold AND mint. Every other `em` on the page (h1, method h3, cv-role, footer heading) correctly follows the documented rule: `font-style:normal; font-weight:300`. A careful reader (a peer evaluating the research) is exactly who reads abstracts closely enough to notice, and inconsistency here undercuts the "precise" brand adjective on a site about precision.
- **Fix**: Restyle `.p-abstract em` to match the rest of the system: `font-style: normal; font-weight: 300`, drop the bold.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Two ungrouped option lists exceed the ≤4-per-group cognitive-load guideline.**
- **Why it matters**: `.hero-chips` shows 5 tags with no grouping; the "Tools & methods" `.pill-row` shows 8 ungrouped pills (Python, R, Stata, LaTeX, oTree, z-Tree, HTML/CSS, Bootstrap). Both ask a first-time visitor to parse a flat list past the point where grouping would help.
- **Fix**: Sub-group the Tools row (e.g., "Software" vs. "Platforms/frameworks"); trim or group the hero chips.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] Paper index numbering disappears entirely on mobile.**
- **Why it matters**: `.p-num { display: none }` at line 293 hides the circled-numeral/specimen-index motif — part of the site's identity — for the majority-mobile audience.
- **Fix**: Keep a compact version of the index number visible on mobile rather than hiding it outright.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P3] Two detector warnings worth a glance.**
- **Why it matters**: `overused-font` (Inter is the only Google Font loaded — likely a low-signal warning given the system deliberately uses Inter for body text) and `em-dash-overuse` (9 em-dashes in body copy) were both flagged. Neither is severe, but the em-dash count is worth a skim for repetitive sentence rhythm.
- **Fix**: Spot-check body copy for em-dash overuse; the font warning is likely a non-issue given the documented type system, but confirm no unintended second Google Font is loading.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`
